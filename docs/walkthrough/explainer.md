# STRATA explainer walkthrough

The approach, and how the approved plan lands in STRATA, in ten short chapters. The player spotlights the part of the screen each chapter talks about and performs the changes it describes.

1. **What it is.** STRATA is the layer the approved plan lands in. Sia builds the 2027 to 2031 plan for Saudi Lime Industries in ten steps: four of analysis, six of business plan building. Nothing from the analysis is reworked in the plan, and nothing from the plan is rebuilt here. The same facts, shifts, objectives, scorecard, initiatives, projects and plans that the Board approves are loaded into this cockpit, computed live from six levers, and every number can be traced back to the rule that produced it. All data here is illustrative.
2. **From facts to objectives.** Strategic direction opens on the cascade. The shift agenda from Phase 1 sits at the top: ten shifts, each on one of the chairman pathways and decided against the levers it depends on. Open a shift and its objectives appear with an owner, a target and a status flag: on track, at risk, or unfunded when nothing that feeds it is in the plan. Hover a shift and the three strata below light up: the assumptions it touches, the initiatives that serve its objectives, and the plan lines that move.
3. **Scorecard.** The Scorecard is the performance framework, step six of the approach. Twenty KPIs across the four balanced scorecard perspectives, each with its 2026 baseline and its targets to 2031. Where the engine computes the value, the KPI reads live and a chip shows the gap to target. Lead indicators are marked: steel consumption, the energy index and the carbon price are the readings the triggers use. Below the grid, every objective lists its OKRs. The framework exists before the initiatives, so the initiatives are measured against it.
4. **Growth portfolio and projects.** The Growth portfolio holds eighteen initiatives in three lanes: in plan, deferred, and out. Every card names the objectives it serves and the projects under it, with capex, NPV, EBITDA, the owner and the lever value that would change its status. Watch: as energy passes 130, the PCC plant fails its rule and slides from Deferred to Out. Open a card for the full sheet: the cascade breadcrumb from shift to objective to initiative, the two to four projects with dates and capex, and what the initiative needs to work.
5. **Plans and requirements.** Plans and requirements is step eight. The projects feed five functional plans: Commercial, Operations and supply chain, Digital and data, Sustainability, and HR and organization. Each card lists its projects with status, capex by year, the headcount it adds or removes, and what the plan needs to work: people, systems and the decisions still to be taken. With energy at 140 the Operations plan loses the PCC projects, and the requirements shrink with them. Each card names the deliverable it serves in the proposal.
6. **Financial plan from the baseline.** The Financial plan is the dynamic model, step nine, starting from the accepted baseline. Five years of history sit in grey to the left of the 2026 divider, with the previous plan target marked at 2024, then the plan. 2026 is shown twice: the actual on diesel and crude, and the pro forma on gas the plan starts from, because a baseline on diesel is the wrong starting point for a plan on gas. Set the capital envelope to Derived and the model proposes one from its own cash. Move energy to 140 and the whole model re-settles: the gas allocation slips a year, EBITDA compresses, the delta chips turn coral against Base.
7. **Roadmap.** The Roadmap draws the projects under their initiatives on a quarterly timeline in the five layers of the plan. The initiative bar is the group header; thin bars beneath it are its projects. Ghost bars are viable initiatives waiting on capital, with the envelope they need. Dependencies run between initiatives, milestones mark ramp completion, and the critical path is in teal.
8. **Tracker and the quarterly review.** The Tracker is what SLIC owns after handover, Phase 3. Type the actual for an elapsed year and the engine re-runs with it in place. The scorecard KPIs sit under the levers against their 2027 targets, and the lead indicators take a typed actual. The triggers-fired panel lists the initiatives whose status changes and the decision now due. Switch to Quarterly review, pick a quarter, and read the KPIs due, the triggers evaluated and the decisions due.
9. **Every number explains itself.** Every number can explain itself. Switch on Explain this result and a slash appears beside every KPI, card and site. Click it for the rules and assumptions behind the value, with the lever values applied, and the cascade it sits in: lever, shift, objective, initiative, project, plan line, KPI. Export PDF prints the whole plan, scorecard included, with the ten steps on the first page.
10. **Make it live.** That is STRATA. The approved plan, loaded as data, tested under every future, and handed over as a living plan. After handover the Finance Department owns the financial model and the Organizational Excellence Department owns the tracker and the quarterly cycle. It runs as static files on an SLIC web server, a SharePoint library or any static host, with no licence. Illustrative data throughout, scaled to a company of SLIC's size: the numbers change when the data does. Powered by Sia.
