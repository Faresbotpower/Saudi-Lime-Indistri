# STRATA explainer walkthrough

What the app is and how it works, in eleven short chapters. Each line is one chapter; the player spotlights the part of the screen it talks about.

1. STRATA is a dynamic strategy cockpit, built by Sia for Saudi Lime Industries and its 2027 to 2031 strategic business plan. Most strategy plans are documents, fixed on the day they are printed. STRATA is a system. The plan is computed live from six levers, and every number on screen can be traced back to the lever and the rule that produced it. All data here is illustrative.
2. On the left are the six levers the Board controls: domestic demand outlook, energy cost, capital envelope, risk appetite, export ambition and carbon cost. Each card shows the current value, what it moves, and the assumptions it touches. Four presets, Base, Growth, Upside and Downside, set all six at once. Move any lever and the whole plan recomputes in a few milliseconds.
3. Behind the screen is a ten-step engine. It builds demand by sector, checks it against site capacity, moves prices with utilization, and builds cost per ton from energy and carbon. It then tests every initiative against its rules, selects a portfolio within the capital envelope, and consolidates everything into a five-year P and L, a classification of every market, and a roadmap. The engine is plain code with no license to buy, and every number lives in two data files a consultant can edit.
4. The Financial plan view is the first thing a CFO wants to see. Four headline numbers for 2031, revenue and EBITDA against the Base case as dashed lines, capex and free cash flow by year, and the four presets side by side. When a lever moves, the numbers count to their new values, and delta chips show the change against Base: teal when favourable, coral when not.
5. The Growth portfolio holds sixteen initiatives in three columns: in plan, deferred, and out. Every card shows capex, NPV, EBITDA, the owner, and a trigger point: the lever value that would change its status. A capital bar shows what the envelope funds. Click a card for the full initiative sheet: objective, rationale, impact, timeline, risks and KPIs.
6. Strategic direction is the argument of the whole product. Three layers: assumptions, initiatives, plan. Hover a lever and the layers light up top to bottom, showing which assumptions it touches, which initiatives those affect, and which plan lines change. Below, a where-to-play matrix places every product market by attractiveness and position, and a table gives each one its category and the reason.
7. Operations and people shows the three sites, Riyadh, Al Kharj and Jeddah, with a utilization gauge, capacity, capex phasing and the initiatives at each site, for any year on the scrubber. Below it, headcount, Saudization against the Nitaqat target, cost per ton, and the unit costs the levers set directly.
8. The Roadmap lays the funded initiatives on a quarterly timeline in the five layers of the plan, from quick wins to diversification. Ghost bars show viable initiatives waiting on capital and how much more envelope they need. Dependencies are drawn between bars, milestones mark ramp completion, and the critical path is in teal.
9. The Tracker is what SLIC owns after handover. Type the actual for an elapsed year and the engine re-runs with it in place. The triggers-fired panel lists the initiatives whose status changes and the decision now due. The same rules, run on real numbers.
10. Every number can explain itself. Switch on Explain this result and a small slash appears beside every KPI, card and site. Click it for the rules and assumptions behind that value, with the lever values applied. Traceable in two clicks, as the RFQ asks.
11. That is STRATA. Six levers, one engine, six views, and every number traceable. Move a lever and the plan re-settles. Illustrative data throughout: the numbers change when the data does. Powered by Sia.
